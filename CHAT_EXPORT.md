# PART 1 — UNDERSTAND WHAT MAVE IS

MAVE stands for our vision of a personal, local-first AI intelligence ecosystem.

Our tagline is:

**MAVE — A Little Friend for Everything.**

MAVE is designed to become much more than a chatbot.

The intended product is a personal AI companion that:

- Runs on users' own devices wherever technically practical.
- Remembers relevant context, preferences, knowledge, goals, and past interactions.
- Understands voice, text, screens, images, applications, and authorized devices.
- Plans and executes real tasks.
- Can control applications and computers with appropriate permissions.
- Connects existing AI models, agents, services, and applications.
- Discovers, acquires, creates, and validates new skills.
- Uses specialized AI models when the local core model is insufficient.
- Works as a personal assistant, productivity system, teacher, and workplace assistant.
- Can connect IoT devices and home automation systems.
- Can potentially host multiple independent users.
- Can become a personal server and private knowledge system.
- Can eventually integrate Docker environments, virtual machines, remote desktop streaming, and shared computation.

We are exploring a **small local intelligence core with dynamically expandable capabilities**.

The conceptual approach is similar to how a capable person works:

1. Understand the request.
2. Observe relevant information.
3. Determine what is known and unknown.
4. Ask questions when necessary.
5. Research or learn the missing information.
6. Choose appropriate tools and skills.
7. Plan the work.
8. Execute actions.
9. Check the result.
10. Remember useful knowledge for future tasks.

The goal is not to build an AI that already knows everything.

The goal is to build a lightweight coordinating intelligence that can obtain the capabilities it needs.

**THIS IS THE PRODUCT VISION, NOT A CLAIM THAT THE CURRENT CODEBASE ALREADY IMPLEMENTS THESE FEATURES.**

Your most important responsibility is identifying the difference between our vision and the actual existing implementation.

Runaway processes exceeding memory budgets or execution timeouts are forcefully terminated (`SIGTERM` followed by `SIGKILL`).
